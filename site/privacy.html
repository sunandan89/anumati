Anumati Collect privacy policy

Last updated 27 September 2026

Anumati Collect is an app that field workers of nonprofit organisations use to ask people for consent under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. It is open-source software. Each organisation runs its own Anumati server, and that organisation is the data fiduciary for everything collected with the app. The Anumati project does not receive or see this data.

Who uses the app

Only staff and volunteers whom an organisation has given a user account. The app is not for the general public and not for children.

What the app collects

  • About the person giving consent: a reference number, name, mobile number (optional), preferred language, and whether they need help reading, share a phone, have no phone, are under 18, or have a lawful guardian.
  • Their choices: which uses of their data they agreed to or refused, the notice version they were shown, and when.
  • Evidence of consent, when the person was helped: a short voice recording of their agreement, a photo of a thumbprint on a paper slip, a witness’s name, and, for a guardian, a photo of an ID or court order.
  • About the field worker: their user account and a random ID for the phone.

The app uses the camera and microphone only when the field worker taps to take a photo or record. It does not collect location, contacts or other files, and it does not send text messages itself: to confirm a phone number it opens the SMS app with a message ready, and the field worker chooses to send it.

Why

To record consent in a way that can be proven later, to let people withdraw it, and to stop the organisation from using data for purposes a person refused. Nothing is used for advertising or sold.

Where it goes

  • On the phone, data is kept in an encrypted database and encrypted files, with keys that never leave the phone. Screenshots of the app are blocked and it is excluded from phone backups.
  • When the phone has internet, records are sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) to the organisation’s own Anumati server. There, names and phone numbers are stored encrypted, and each consent is digitally signed so it can’t be altered unnoticed.
  • Evidence files are removed from the phone once they reach the server. Signing out of the app deletes everything the app stored on the phone.
  • Data is not shared with the Anumati project or any third party by the app. The organisation’s own policy says whether it shares data with partners, and its consent notice lists them.

How long

The organisation decides retention for each purpose and shows it in its consent notice. Consent records themselves are kept for as long as the organisation needs to prove consent.

Your rights

If you gave consent through this app, you can withdraw it at any time: reply STOP with the code on your receipt to the organisation’s SMS number, give it a missed call, or tell any of its workers. You can also ask to see, correct or delete your data, or make a complaint. Contact the organisation’s Data Protection Officer, named in the notice and on your receipt. If you are not satisfied, you can complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Contact

For questions about your data, contact the organisation that collected it. For questions about the software, open an issue at github.com/sunandan89/anumati_collect.
